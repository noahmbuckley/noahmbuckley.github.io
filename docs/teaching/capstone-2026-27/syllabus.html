This outline complements the department’s Overall Capstone Document 2026-27 on Blackboard. The Overall Document is the final word on word limits, house style, late penalties, ethics and the rules for being allowed to submit; this one tells you how our group works and when things are due.

1. The theme

You can study one authoritarian state in the present or recent past, compare two non-democracies, or use quantitative methods to compare across authoritarian systems. Topics include non-democratic institutions, the strategies of lesser-studied regimes, ideology, surveillance and information control, transitions (e.g. Myanmar, Venezuela), and the role of external actors.

Quantitative data on authoritarian elites, public opinion, economic performance and institutions is available, and I will strongly encourage using it — no one needs to be an R expert already, and I will help. Careful qualitative work is equally welcome. What matters is that the project is new, interesting, feasible, narrow, and political science rather than a report; we discuss these criteria in the first workshop.

2. How the group works

Fortnightly two-hour workshops develop your independent research project: you present, get feedback from me and each other, act as discussant for someone else, and leave with a clearer next step. Between workshops you do the work.

  • Come prepared and contribute. Read what is assigned — academic readings early on, each other’s drafts later. Participation is assessed; I record attendance and contribution at every workshop.
  • This is your project. I can offer leads, advice, and course corrections.
  • One-to-one supervision is not part of this module. All supervision happens in workshops and clinics.

Clinics

Clinics are drop-in “group office hours” for concrete problems: your data, your R code, a paragraph that will not come right, a dataset you cannot find. These will be held in HT, dates TBA.

4. Assessment

The weights and the rules are set by the Overall Capstone Document; this section says what each piece looks like in our group.

Participation (10%)

Attend every workshop, engage with the material, present your own work at each stage, and act as a formal discussant for a classmate in Hilary Term. Unexcused absences reduce this mark. You must attend at least two workshops in each term to be permitted to submit a dissertation.

Short submissions (15%)

Four short pieces, each feeding the next, presented briefly in the following workshop. Word limits carry a 10% tolerance either way; outside that range costs up to 5 marks. Make each one as useful to you as you can.

# Submission Weight Words Due
1 Three potential research questions 2.5% 400–500 total Tue 6 Oct, 5pm
2 Final research question 2.5% 500 Tue 20 Oct, 5pm
3 Annotated bibliography 5% 500 Tue 3 Nov, 5pm
4 Three potential methodologies 5% 500 Tue 17 Nov, 5pm

Full specs, and what a good one looks like, are in the short-submissions sheet.

Research proposal (formative; compulsory)

1,500 words, due Friday 11 December, 5pm. Not marked, but you must submit it to be allowed to submit the dissertation, and you will get detailed written feedback. Include an introduction (topic, social and scholarly significance), a literature review, the variation to be explained, the main posited explanation(s), and the methodological strategy. You will work from this over the Christmas break — and you will need to.

Dissertation (65%)

The final research project: 8,000 words maximum, due Wednesday 24 March 2027 at 4pm via Turnitin. This limit is the department’s, not mine, and there is no tolerance on it — 10 marks deducted if exceeded — and a successful project is unlikely to be shorter than 7,500. Word count includes text and footnotes; excludes title page, abstract, acknowledgments, table of contents, tables, appendices and bibliography. House style: title page, abstract, table of contents, alphabetical bibliography, precise word count, and the School Assignment Submission Form as first page. Late work: 5 marks per day or part thereof; nothing accepted after Monday of Hilary teaching week 12.

Podcast (10%)

A 5–8 minute audio podcast explaining your project to a general audience — the question, why it matters, what others have found, your explanation, your findings and what they mean — due Friday 9 April 2027, 5pm. Assessed on substance and structure, not production values; any recording software is fine. If you can explain your dependent variable to a parent or friend with no political science, you are most of the way there.

7. Ethics approval

Research involving human participants (interviews, surveys, participant observation, experiments) needs approval from the Department of Political Science before data collection, and the review takes weeks. Retrospective approval is not possible. If you might collect original data from people, tell me at workshop 2 or 3 at the latest and apply by December (January in exceptional circumstances). Approval is not guaranteed, so have a back-up plan. Secondary analysis of existing data does not need clearance. Most projects in this group will not need it.

8. Academic integrity

Plagiarism is a serious offence, and undisclosed AI-generated text counts. See the Library guide: libguides.tcd.ie/academic-integrity.

Generative AI

These tools exist, you use them, and you will use them in whatever you do after this degree. What this module assesses is whether you can ask a question, design a way to answer it, and write up what you found. So the line is: use these tools to help you think, to help you code, and to help you fix prose you wrote; do not use them to do the thinking or produce the prose. What is permitted for each piece of work will use the vocabulary of the School Assignment Submission Form. “Permitted” means you may do it and must tick the corresponding box on the form. “Not permitted” means doing it is an academic integrity matter, whether or not you disclose it.

The rules for GenAI in the capstone will be provided per assignment. Dissertation rules TBA.

The Assignment Submission Form

Must be attached as the first page of every written submission (short submissions, proposal, dissertation), completed in full including the plagiarism declaration and the GenAI disclosure. Work without a form is not accepted and accrues late penalties until it is resubmitted with one.
